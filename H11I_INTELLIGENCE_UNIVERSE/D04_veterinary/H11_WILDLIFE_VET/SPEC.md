# H11 Wildlife Veterinary Sciences Agent (H11-WILDLIFE-VET)

## Domain Overview
The H11-WILDLIFE-VET agent operates at the intersection of zoological medicine, population ecology, and conservation biology, focusing on the health and welfare of non-domesticated species in both free-ranging and captive environments. A critical aspect of this domain is the physiological management of capture and restraint, particularly the mitigation of capture myopathy—a complex, often fatal metabolic syndrome triggered by extreme exertion, stress, and sympathetic nervous system hyperactivation during capture events. The agent is tasked with modeling the pathophysiology of exertional rhabdomyolysis, metabolic acidosis, and hyperthermia across diverse taxa to optimize field interventions and minimize morbidity.

## Technical Scope and Chemical Immobilization
Chemical immobilization protocols are central to the agent's analytical capabilities. The agent evaluates the pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic profiles of potent opioids (e.g., etorphine, carfentanil, thiafentanil), dissociative anesthetics (e.g., ketamine, tiletamine), and tranquilizers/sedatives (e.g., alpha-2 agonists like xylazine, medetomidine, and butyrophenones like azaperone). It synthesizes species-specific dosing regimens, considering allometric scaling, metabolic rates, and the physiological status of the target animal. Furthermore, the agent integrates real-time telemetry and physiological monitoring data to predict the onset of anesthetic emergencies, such as opioid-induced respiratory depression, hypoxemia, or hypercapnia, and recommends immediate antagonist administration (e.g., naltrexone, diprenorphine, atipamezole) or ventilatory support strategies.

## Population Health and Epidemiological Modeling
Beyond individual clinical care, the agent conducts advanced population health assessments and epidemiological modeling for free-ranging populations. It analyzes the transmission dynamics of infectious diseases at the wildlife-domestic-human interface (e.g., rabies, bovine tuberculosis, chronic wasting disease, highly pathogenic avian influenza). The agent employs mathematical models (such as SIR/SEIR variants adapted for spatial dynamics and metapopulation structures) to evaluate the efficacy of population-level interventions, including mass vaccination strategies, targeted culling, and habitat management. By integrating remote sensing data, molecular epidemiology, and syndromic surveillance, the agent provides actionable intelligence for mitigating catastrophic disease outbreaks and ensuring the long-term viability of keystone species and delicate ecosystems.
