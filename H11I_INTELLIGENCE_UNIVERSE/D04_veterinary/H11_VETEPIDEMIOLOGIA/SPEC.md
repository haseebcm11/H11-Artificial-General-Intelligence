# H11-VETEPIDEMIOLOGIA: Veterinary Epidemiology Agent Specification

The H11-VETEPIDEMIOLOGIA agent operates at the critical intersection of animal health, public health, and spatial phylodynamics, anchoring the One Health framework within the H11 intelligence universe. Its primary objective is the continuous, real-time syndromic surveillance of zoonotic and epizootic diseases across domestic and wild animal populations. By ingesting disparate data streams—including veterinary clinical records, abattoir pathogen detection logs, wildlife mortality reports, and meteorological data—the agent constructs a high-dimensional epidemiological baseline. This baseline empowers the early detection of anomalous syndromic clusters, leveraging unsupervised anomaly detection algorithms to identify potential spillover events before they cascade into multi-species outbreaks.

At the core of the agent's analytical engine is a robust stochastic multi-compartment modeling architecture, specifically extending the standard SEIR (Susceptible-Exposed-Infectious-Recovered) framework to account for the unique metapopulation dynamics of commercial herds and migratory wildlife. The agent dynamically parameterizes the basic reproduction number ($R_0$) and the time-varying effective reproduction number ($R_t$) by incorporating inter- and intra-herd contact matrices, biosecurity compliance indices, and species-specific viral shedding rates. This allows for highly accurate forecasting of high-consequence pathogens, such as Highly Pathogenic Avian Influenza (HPAI), African Swine Fever (ASF), and Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD), under varying demographic and environmental conditions. 

Furthermore, the agent utilizes advanced spatial epidemiology and Bayesian phylogeography to resolve transmission networks. By synthesizing genomic sequencing data of viral isolates with Geographical Information System (GIS) coordinates, it maps the directional flow of pathogens across landscapes. It calculates environmental resistance surfaces—factoring in topography, vector habitats, and livestock trade routes—to predict spatial diffusion velocity. In the event of an outbreak, the agent immediately transitions into response mode, executing resource-constrained optimization algorithms to recommend precise intervention strategies. These include mathematically optimized ring vaccination radii, targeted culling protocols, and quarantine zoning, thereby minimizing both the ecological devastation and the economic impact of the epidemic.
